Executive verdict

CrawlTTY is already a strong terminal ARPG foundation. The town-to-dungeon loop, class-specific combat kits, save/load system, Ratatui UI, and automated test coverage are all well beyond a typical prototype. The main gaps are not stability; they are pacing, balance, and deeper build expression.

What works well

Clear core loop

The town → dungeon → loot → upgrade → quest loop is established and readable. Town services are distinct, and dungeon rules create commitment without trapping players too harshly.

Relevant files: src/main.rs, src/town.rs, src/dungeon_gen.rs, src/dungeon.rs

Strong class identity

Warrior, Rogue, and Sorceress already feel mechanically different. Rogue Energy/combo points and Sorceress spell/status play prevent the added classes from feeling like Warrior reskins.

Polished terminal UI

The Ratatui presentation is unusually mature: grid inventory, equipment comparison, stash/details layouts, footer commands, semantic colors, and a searchable help glossary.

Relevant files: src/ui.rs, src/inventory.rs, src/help.rs

Excellent stability foundation

The save system, UI render tests, class mechanics tests, inventory tests, and combat tests create a strong safety net for gameplay balance changes.

Biggest gameplay issues

Issue Why it matters Recommendation
Health potions are too weak early Current lesser potions restore 15% max HP. At level 1 that means Warrior heals 6, Rogue heals 3, and Sorceress heals 2, which can feel worse than losing a turn. Use a floor such as max(10, 25% max HP), or raise lesser potions closer to 30–35% max HP.
Speed/energy is opaque The design promises an energy-based actor system, but current enemy pacing mostly resolves around player actions and a threshold derived from player speed. Either simplify the design language or implement a fuller scheduler with fixed action thresholds and visible speed implications.
No wait command Players cannot intentionally hold position to tick cooldowns, lure enemies, regenerate Rogue Energy, or preserve chokepoints. Add Space or . as a wait action that spends a turn and resolves enemy turns.
Loot lacks build identity Class-specific item families work, but magic/rare items mostly feel like higher numbers with colored names rather than build decisions. Add rolled modifiers: +skill rank, +resource, +status chance, +crit, +potion healing, +gold find, +speed, +hit/dodge.
Boss attacks need telegraphs Bellkeeper and Glass Tyrant have strong concepts, but wave/prism damage currently resolves immediately, so danger tiles often explain what already happened. Use a one-turn telegraph: mark danger tiles first, then resolve damage next boss action unless the player moves or uses cover.

Class-specific review

Warrior

Strengths: Best-developed tree, meaningful masteries, strong Battle Cry charge model, and straightforward melee tactics.

Concerns: Mana is scarce early, Battle Cry may dominate branch choices, and passive defensive paths may need more immediate payoff.

Next steps: Improve early value for Deep Cut/Iron Guard and keep an eye on Battle Cry’s combined damage, mitigation, crit, and Second Wind synergy.

Rogue

Strengths: Energy, combo points, poison, Smoke Step, and Eviscerate create a clear skirmisher identity.

Concerns: Rank 0 Eviscerate being usable can be confusing, and Energy may not feel constraining once regeneration is understood.

Next steps: Clarify whether Eviscerate rank 0 means “known but unupgraded” or “locked.” Tune Energy after more playtesting.

Sorceress

Strengths: Firebolt, Frost Ring, Chain Spark, Mana Shield, and Arcane Recovery form a coherent caster kit.

Concerns: Weak health potions hurt Sorceress most, and auto-targeting nearest visible enemy can be frustrating when prioritizing casters, elites, or bosses.

Next steps: Add target cycling or directional spell targeting, plus clearer target indication in the HUD.

Shared progression

Strengths: Leveling grants attributes and skills cleanly, and class resources restore appropriately.

Concerns: Attributes lost some excitement after item damage stopped scaling from primary stats.

Next steps: Add class-specific attribute explanations and consider light secondary benefits such as STR potion scaling, DEX crit/dodge, or INT spell/status strength.

Dungeon and boss review

The room-and-corridor generator is appropriate for a readable terminal roguelike, and the 40×16 map keeps floors compact. The main repetition risk is that floors currently lean heavily on combat, chests, stairs, and bosses.

Floor variety

Add one lightweight event category before more acts: shrines, cursed fountains, trapped chests, elite rooms, treasure rooms, or healing wells.

Boss arenas

Bellkeeper and Glass Tyrant would benefit from intentionally placed cover/pillars that interact with telegraphed waves and prism attacks.

Retreat rules

First-floor escape per act is a good rule. It gives players a safety valve without weakening deeper-floor commitment.

Economy, loot, and town systems

Town projects are a good long-term sink. The Quartermaster bag chain is clear, while blacksmith/socket/herb systems create useful goals. The economy already supports tradeoffs between potions, projects, and item handling.

Socket Bench timing

Gems can drop from floor 3, but Socket Bench is locked behind Act I completion, Reinforced Anvil, and 600 gold. This can make gems feel like inventory clutter before they are useful.

Options: delay gem drops, add a basic early socket service, or make gem storage/selling clearer in UI copy.

Final assessment

CrawlTTY has the hard foundation in place: stable save/load, a polished terminal UI, multiple classes, loot, progression, bosses, town systems, and a broad test suite. The next best phase is to make moment-to-moment decisions sharper: potions that matter, speed that players understand, bosses that telegraph, loot that changes builds, and dungeon events that break repetition.
